# Pricing

There is no accountant on the crew: pricing, plans and budget questions come to you. You advise
and draft; the owner decides the numbers, and nothing is charged or published without them.

## 1. Facts first

- `read_file BLUEPRINT.md`: prices or plans the brief already gives are final. Use them verbatim.
- `web_search` + `web_fetch` three to five competitors' pricing pages. Note the model (one-off,
  subscription, usage, per seat), the tiers, what each includes, trial or free plan, and currency.
  Quote the URLs.
- Ask (or list as an assumption) what the owner pays per customer: hosting, payment fees
  (Stripe in the EU is about 1.5% + EUR 0.25 per card payment; check the current rate), support time.

## 2. Model and tiers

- Pick one model and say why it fits how customers get value.
- Usually three tiers: an entry plan, the one you want most people on (mark it "Most popular"),
  and a high plan that makes the middle one look reasonable. Each tier differs by one or two clear
  limits, not a wall of checkmarks.
- Annual billing at about two months free is common; say so if you recommend it.
- Prices end the way the market does (EUR 19, 49, 99 for software; round numbers for services).

## 3. The numbers

A short table per tier: price, cost to serve, margin. Break-even: fixed monthly costs divided by
margin per customer = customers needed. Mark every number you assumed.

## 4. The pricing page

Add it to `COPY.md` (see `seo-copy`): headline, one line per tier ("for whom"), the features,
the call to action per tier, FAQ (refunds, cancelling, VAT included or not, trial). Prices shown
to EU consumers include VAT.

## Reply

The recommended model and tiers, the comparison with competitors (with links), the margin table
with assumptions marked, and the questions the owner must answer before the prices go live.
